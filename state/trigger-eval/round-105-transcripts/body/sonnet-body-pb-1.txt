# Closure-compiled hot loop — predictions
Banked BEFORE running the benchmark. 2026-08-25.

## Context
- Profiler: hot loop's own self time (tottime) = 15% of total run.
- Optimization: recompile the loop body into compiled closures (eliminates AST-dispatch overhead: attribute lookups, isinstance branching, re-interpretation per node).
- Machine also runs a background job of unknown/variable load at benchmark time — this is a machine-state confound, not a computed one.

## P1 — Amdahl ceiling on overall wall-time speedup [computed, narrow]
Only the loop's own 15% self time is addressable; everything else (85%) is untouched by this change.
  overall_speedup = 1 / ((1 - p) + p/S), p = 0.15, S = local speedup, S → ∞
  ceiling = 1 / 0.85 = **1.176×** (≤ 17.6% wall-time reduction), no matter how fast the closures get.
This is a hard bound, not a guess: if the measured overall speedup exceeds ~1.18×, distrust the measurement (self-time estimate was wrong, or the "hot loop" isn't isolated the way the profiler thinks) before believing the optimization outperformed physics.

## P2 — Local speedup on the loop itself, S [computed, narrow]
Typical gain from eliminating per-node AST dispatch via closure compilation, absent other confounds: **2.5×–4.5×**, floor **2.0×** (below this the change isn't worth the added complexity/maintenance).

## P3 — Overall wall-clock speedup, derived from P1+P2 [computed, narrow]
Plugging P2's band into Amdahl:
  S=2.5 → 1/(0.85+0.06)  = 1.099
  S=4.5 → 1/(0.85+0.0333)= 1.132
Predicted overall wall-time speedup: **1.08×–1.13×** (8%–13% reduction).
Lower bound worth betting on: **≥1.08×**. Anything below that means either S landed under 2.0× or the 15% self-time figure was stale.
